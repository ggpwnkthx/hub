# Hub — Security

The security model of the service and the review of it, item by item. Every
item is answered with a fact: a test, a measurement or a piece of code.

The rules that the service obeys are in [spec.md](spec.md) §2.1 (the
invariants) and §3.9 (the isolation of the tenants). What follows is the
review of those rules and of everything around them.

## The model

| The surface | What protects it |
| --- | --- |
| The login of a browser | The state of the login is encrypted, authenticated and single use; the code is opaque, stored under a hash, single use and bound to the client, to the redirect uri and to the PKCE challenge; the token of the provider is validated before a single row is read. |
| The decisions | The gateway authenticates itself with a bearer token or with the credentials of its tenant; the comparison is in constant time; the rules of the tenants are enforced by the database (row level security). The bearer token of the deployment is optional and deprecated: a deployment that does not set it accepts only the credentials of a tenant, which are scoped to it. |
| The administration | One bearer token for the whole platform, or a credential per tenant, compared in constant time, with a rate limit per address; every call is audited in a table; the secrets are write-only. |
| The secrets at rest | The two client secrets of a tenant are encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key that is derived with HKDF-SHA256 from the secret of the deployment. |
| The image | A user that is not `root`, no shell, no tool of the build, a base image pinned by digest, a scan that fails the build above "high", an SBOM. |

## The review

| # | What was reviewed | Result | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The enumeration of the identifiers.** The administrative endpoints answer the id of the thing they created: they are guarded by the token of the administration. The decisions answer `the principal does not exist` and `the principal does not have the role of the workspace`: the difference tells a caller that a subject exists. The caller of the decisions is the gateway, which is trusted, and the gateway hides the reason. | Accepted | `src/domains/authorization/decision.ts` |
| 2 | **The timing of the answers.** The tokens of the administration, of the decisions and of the metrics are compared in constant time: the two values are hashed first, so the comparison does not depend on the length of the secret. The state of a login is authenticated: a state that was changed does not decrypt. | Fixed | `src/observability/secrets.ts`, `src/domains/identity/state.ts` |
| 3 | **The injection of SQL.** Every statement is parameterised. The identifiers of the tables and of the columns are constants of the code: no value of a caller ever reaches a statement as text. | No finding | `src/platform/postgres/` |
| 4 | **The SSRF of the `idp_issuer`.** A tenant registers the issuer of its provider and Hub calls that address. The address is checked before the call: the host is resolved and every address it resolves to has to be a public one. A production deployment refuses the private addresses; a development machine accepts them (the fixtures of the tests are on the loopback). | Fixed | `src/platform/bun/address.ts` |
| 5 | **The redirect of the provider.** The calls to the providers of the tenants do not follow a redirect: a provider that answers with a redirect cannot send the credentials of the deployment somewhere else. | Fixed | `src/platform/bun/http.ts` |
| 6 | **The XSS of the refusal pages.** The pages of the refusals of the login escape everything they show. | Fixed | `src/app/pages.ts` |
| 7 | **The secrets in the logs.** The logger redacts a token, a secret, a cookie, a password and an `Authorization` header wherever they appear, in the fields and in the text of a message. The values of a statement are never written: only the statement itself. | Fixed | `src/observability/logging.ts`, `src/platform/postgres/trace.ts` |
| 8 | **The secrets at rest.** The two client secrets of a tenant are encrypted with AES-256-GCM under `HUB_SECRET_ENCRYPTION_KEY` before they are written. A service without a key refuses to write a tenant: it never stores a secret in clear. | Fixed | `src/platform/postgres/secrets.ts` |
| 9 | **The state of a login.** It is encrypted, it is authenticated, it is single use (it is deleted whatever the outcome of the callback is) and it lives ten minutes. | Fixed | `src/domains/identity/state.ts` |
| 10 | **The authorization code.** It is opaque, it is stored under a hash of itself, it lives sixty seconds, it is single use and it is bound to the client, to the redirect uri and to the challenge of the PKCE. | Fixed | `src/domains/provider/code.ts`, `src/domains/provider/exchange.ts` |
| 11 | **The failure is a denial.** A database that is down, a store that is down, a provider that is slow and a decision that does not answer in time are all denials: the gateway fails closed on anything that is not a successful answer. | Fixed | `src/app/decisions.ts` |
| 12 | **The reason never reaches a caller.** The reasons are for the operator and they are in the log of the decision. The answer of the decision carries the reason because the contract of the gateway asks for it, and the gateway hides it from the caller. | Accepted | `src/app/decisions.ts` |
| 13 | **The image.** It runs as a user that is not `root`, it has no shell and it carries no tool of the build. The base image is pinned by digest. | Fixed | `Dockerfile` |
| 14 | **The password of the database.** It is not in the image: it comes from the environment, and the rotation is the deployment of a new secret. | No finding | `src/runtime/env.ts` |
| 15 | **The rate limit of the administration.** A burst of failed authentications is refused with `429`, and an address that used up its budget is refused whatever it presents. The count of the failures is held by the shared store of the deployment, so the window of the limiter is the window of the deployment and not the window of a replica. | Fixed | `src/domains/administration/limiter.ts`, `src/platform/redis/store.ts` |
| 16 | **The limits of the process.** The body of an administrative call is at most 64 KiB, the calls to the providers have a deadline (`HUB_IDP_TIMEOUT_MS`) and the decisions have a deadline (`HUB_DECISION_TIMEOUT_MS`). | Fixed | `src/app/administration.ts`, `src/domains/identity/provider-client.ts` |
| 17 | **The redirection of the browser.** The redirect uri of the gateway is a value the caller controls, so it is checked against the ones the gateway is registered with before the tenant is resolved. A url that is not registered is refused with `400 invalid_request` and the browser is not sent to it: the refusal is a page of Hub, so a url that is not registered never receives a code. | Fixed | `src/domains/identity/tenant.ts`, `src/app/login.ts` |
| 18 | **The cookies.** The state of a login is `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Secure` in production and its path is the callback of the login. | Fixed | `src/app/login.ts` |
| 19 | **The credentials of the decisions.** Two schemes are accepted (spec §8.8): the bearer token of the deployment, which is the scheme of the gateway of the reference and is **not scoped**, and the credentials of a tenant, which are scoped to that tenant. A gateway that presents the credentials of a tenant cannot ask about the principals of the other tenants: the answer is the one of a principal that does not exist. | Fixed | `src/app/decisions.ts` |
| 20 | **The isolation of the tenants.** The application isolates the tenants, and the database is the backstop of that isolation: the five tables have the row level security of PostgreSQL and their policy is the tenant of the statement. A statement that forgets the tenant is refused by PostgreSQL itself. The scope is local to the transaction, so a pooled connection never carries the scope of another request. A production deployment refuses to start with a role that bypasses the policies. | Fixed | `src/platform/postgres/scope.ts`, `src/platform/postgres/migrations/0003_tenant_isolation.sql` |
| 21 | **The rotation of the secrets.** A secret that a client presents is rotated without a window in which the client and the service disagree: the secret of the gateway of a tenant is kept for 24 hours when it is replaced, and the tokens of the environment are read from two variables during a rotation. The comparison of the two is in constant time. | Fixed | `src/app/decisions.ts`, `src/app/oauth.ts`, `src/domains/administration/service.ts`, `docs/spec.md` §4.3.1 |
| 22 | **The audit of the administration.** The audit is a table and not a log line: a log line disappears with the container and it is not queryable. The row names the operator by the fingerprint of the credential that made the call, so the audit says who without ever holding the secret. The row is written by the same statement that writes the change, so a change that is not audited does not exist: the two are committed together or neither is. | Fixed | `src/platform/postgres/audited.ts`, `src/platform/postgres/audit.ts`, `src/app/administration.ts` |
| 23 | **The deadline of a statement.** The deadline of a decision is the deadline of the server: the `statement_timeout` of the connection of the pool stops the statement that takes too long and the connection is given back to the pool at once. A deadline that lived only in the process would let a statement hold a connection for the whole window of the timeout of the pool. | Fixed | `src/platform/postgres/postgres.ts`, `src/platform/postgres/decisions.ts` |
| 24 | **The permissions of the tenants.** A key of a permission is a name *inside* a tenant: the unique index is on `(tenant, key)`, so two tenants hold their own row of the same key and a grant never names the row of another tenant. The permission of the catalogue of the platform is shared by every tenant. | Fixed | `src/domains/administration/service.ts`, `src/platform/postgres/permissions.ts` |
| 25 | **The credentials of the administration.** A deployment may name a credential per tenant: the credential may only act for that tenant, and it may carry the name of its operator. A call that names another tenant is a `403 forbidden`, the lists of the credential are the lists of its tenant, and the filter of a query cannot widen them. The audit names the operator and not the fingerprint of a shared secret. | Fixed | `src/domains/administration/credentials.ts`, `src/domains/administration/service.ts`, `src/runtime/composition.ts` |
| 26 | **The flags of a tenant that belong to the platform.** The `shared_client` of a tenant moves the logins of the platform and the `is_default` routes the logins of the shared clients. A credential of a tenant may not change them: a call that carries a value that is not the value of the row is a `403 forbidden`. | Fixed | `src/domains/administration/service.ts` |

## The findings that are open

| # | Finding | Severity | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The reason of a decision tells the gateway whether a subject exists. | Low | The gateway is the only caller and it hides the reason. A reason that says nothing about the existence of a subject would change the vocabulary of spec §8.7 — a change of the specification first. |
| 2 | The bearer token of the decisions is not scoped: the gateway that presents it may ask about any principal of the platform, and the secret is shared by every gateway of the deployment. | Medium | The gateway of the reference only speaks the bearer token, so the scheme cannot be removed without a change of the gateway. Hub already accepts the credentials of a tenant, which are scoped (spec §8.8): a deployment moves its gateways to them and the shared secret loses its power. **The token is now optional**: a deployment that does not set `HUB_ACCESS_TOKEN` accepts only the credentials of a tenant, and the bearer scheme is refused (spec §4.4). The rotation of the token is a rolling change (the token that it replaced is accepted until the deployment removes it, spec §4.3.1), so the change of the environment of every gateway is not simultaneous any more. The token is **deprecated**: the service writes a notice in its log, once, the first time a decision presents it, and the notice names the scheme to use instead. |

No finding is above "low" except the second one, whose plan is a migration
of the deployments and not a change of the service.
